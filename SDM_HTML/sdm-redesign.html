Shared Decision Making

We often rely on our doctors to tell us what care we need. But for the best care, you and your doctor should make decisions together. When there's more than one good way to treat a health problem, the right choice is the one that fits you.

What Is Shared Decision Making?

It's a conversation where you and your doctor decide on care together. It helps most when there's more than one way to address your health issue and no single "right" answer, where your priorities are counted alongside the clinical evidence. You can also compare the costs of each option.

1

Your doctor shares clinical facts

They explain your options and the benefits, risks and costs of each.

2

You share what matters to you

What you want from your care and what you'd rather avoid.

3

You decide together

You choose the option that fits your goals, values and clinical evidence.

When is shared decision making helpful?

Being well-informed about your options can help you plan your care and related costs. FAIR Health offers free decision aids that pair the clinical options from Option Grid™ patient decision aids with FAIR Health cost data. Use them with your healthcare provider—you can review on your own or with a caregiver, print and bring the tool to your next appointment.

The tools include those for "preference-sensitive" conditions, like hip osteoarthritis, uterine fibroids and prostate cancer.

Tools also include those for seriously ill patients receiving palliative care—care that eases pain and discomfort for people who are seriously ill, alongside (not instead of) treatment. FAIR Health offers decision aids for dialysis, nutrition and mechanical ventilation.

Choose from the decision aids on the right.

The decision aids are not intended to be medical advice, diagnosis or treatment. They are meant to help you take part in shared decision making with your health professionals.

Talk through the clinical options in any decision aid with your own health professional, since every patient's condition is different.
